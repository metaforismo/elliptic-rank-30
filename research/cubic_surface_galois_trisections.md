# Cubic-surface models and outer Galois trisections

**Author:** Francesco Giannicola  
**Truth status:** new intermediate theorem; no rank-30 curve is claimed.

## Setting

Let

\[
\pi:S\to\mathbf P^1
\]

be a rational elliptic surface over a field of characteristic zero with zero
section `O`, fibre class `F`, twelve irreducible `I1` fibres, trivial torsion,
and split Mordell--Weil lattice `E8`.

For a positive `E8` vector `v` of norm six, put

\[
D_v=3O+3F+V,
\]

where `V` is the corresponding negative-definite Neron--Severi component. The
complete system `|D_v|` consists generically of genus-one trisections.

## Unique root-pair theorem

Every norm-six vector `v` of `E8` has a unique unordered decomposition

\[
\boxed{v=\alpha+\beta}
\]

with `alpha` and `beta` roots satisfying

\[
\alpha^2=\beta^2=2,
\qquad
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=1.
\]

Indeed, a root `alpha` occurs in such a decomposition exactly when
`<v,alpha>=3`, because then `v-alpha` has norm two. If two distinct roots
have inner product three with `v`, their components perpendicular to `v`
have norm `1/2`; integrality forces their mutual inner product to be one. A
third such root would violate Cauchy--Schwarz. Thus the pair is unique.

The count agrees exactly:

* `E8` has 240 roots;
* each root has 56 roots of inner product one;
* the number of unordered pairs is `240*56/2=6720`;
* the norm-six shell also has 6720 vectors.

The dependency-free certificate enumerates both shells and checks uniqueness
for every vector.

## Three disjoint sections

Let `S_alpha` and `S_beta` be the root sections. Since a root section is
disjoint from `O`, and

\[
S_\alpha\cdot S_\beta
=1-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=0,
\]

the three `(-1)`-curves

\[
O,\qquad S_\alpha,\qquad S_\beta
\]

are pairwise disjoint. Their divisor classes satisfy

\[
S_\alpha=O+F+A,
\qquad
S_\beta=O+F+B,
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
D_v=F+O+S_\alpha+S_\beta.
}
\]

Because `K_S=-F`,

\[
D_v=-K_S+O+S_\alpha+S_\beta.
\]

## Cubic-surface theorem

Contract the three disjoint sections:

\[
\varphi:S\to X.
\]

The canonical divisor transforms as

\[
K_S=\varphi^*K_X+O+S_\alpha+S_\beta,
\]

hence

\[
\boxed{D_v=\varphi^*(-K_X).}
\]

Moreover, `K_X^2=3`. The divisor `D_v` is positive on the fibre and on every
section other than the three contracted ones. For a section with Mordell--Weil
vector `w` of norm `n`,

\[
D_v\cdot S_w
=\frac{3n}{2}-\langle v,w\rangle.
\]

For `n>=4`, Cauchy--Schwarz makes this strictly positive. For `n=2`, equality
occurs only for `alpha` and `beta`. Since the cone of curves is generated by
the fibre and the `(-1)`-sections when all singular fibres are irreducible,
`-K_X` is ample.

Therefore `X` is a smooth degree-three del Pezzo surface, and its
anticanonical system embeds it as a smooth cubic surface in `P3`. The system
`|D_v|` is exactly the pullback of the hyperplane system:

\[
\boxed{|D_v|=\varphi^*|-K_X|.}
\]

This proves base-point-freeness and explains `h^0(D_v)=4` without a coefficient
search.

## The elliptic pencil is projection from a 3-secant line

Let

\[
p_0=\varphi(O),\quad
p_1=\varphi(S_\alpha),\quad
p_2=\varphi(S_\beta).
\]

The fibre pencil `|F|` is the strict transform of anticanonical hyperplanes
through all three points. Since this is a pencil, the three points are
collinear. Let `ell` be their line. The line is not contained in `X`, because
otherwise every member of the pencil would split as that line plus a conic,
contradicting the irreducibility of the generic elliptic fibre. Hence

\[
\ell\cap X=p_0+p_1+p_2
\]

scheme-theoretically.

For a hyperplane `H` not containing `ell`, put

\[
C_H=X\cap H,
\qquad q_H=H\cap\ell.
\]

The strict transform of `C_H` is a member of `|D_v|`. The degree-three map

\[
C_H\to\mathbf P^1
\]

induced by the original elliptic pencil is exactly projection of the plane
cubic `C_H` from the point `q_H`.

Thus minimal trisection search on the elliptic surface is equivalent to a
classical cubic-surface problem:

\[
\boxed{
\text{hyperplane sections of }X
\text{ projected from the moving point }H\cap\ell.
}
\]

## Cyclicity becomes an outer Galois-point condition

Assume `C_H` is smooth. Its projection from `q_H` is a cyclic cubic cover if
and only if `q_H` is an outer Galois point of the plane cubic.

Over an algebraic closure, this is equivalent to the existence of plane
coordinates with

\[
q_H=(1:0:0)
\]

and

\[
\boxed{C_H:\ X^3+G_3(Y,Z)=0,}
\]

where `G_3` is a square-free binary cubic. The deck transformation is

\[
X\mapsto\zeta_3X.
\]

In particular, every smooth cyclic trisection has

\[
\boxed{j(C_H)=0.}
\]

Conversely, a hyperplane section with the displayed outer-Galois form gives a
cyclic degree-three trisection. Arithmetic descent over `Q` remains a separate
condition because the diagonalization occurs naturally over `Q(zeta_3)`.

## Decisive change of setting

The earlier search proposed computing a general four-dimensional
Riemann--Roch system and imposing that an abstract cubic discriminant be a
square. The cubic-surface theorem gives a much sharper representation.

For each norm-six trace class:

1. recover the unique root pair `(alpha,beta)`;
2. contract `O,S_alpha,S_beta` to a marked cubic surface `(X,ell)`;
3. write the cubic form of `X` and the 3-secant line `ell` explicitly;
4. solve for hyperplanes `H` such that `q_H=H cap ell` is an outer Galois
   point of `C_H=X cap H`;
5. test smoothness, rational descent, and positive rank of the resulting
   `j=0` genus-one curve;
6. canonicalize the cover and group distinct trace classes that give the same
   cover;
7. test the Hermitian rank of each common-cover fibre.

This converts the 240-class trace-code search into explicit linear algebra and
plane-cubic invariant equations on marked cubic surfaces. The first decisive
goal is a single cyclic cover produced by two distinct trace classes. Eleven
Hermitian-independent classes over one positive-rank cover would force the
missing twenty-two directions and hence generic rank thirty.
