# Cyclic cubic trisections and the E8 mod-3 trace code

**Author:** Francesco Giannicola  
**Truth status:** new intermediate theorem; no rank-30 curve is claimed.

## Setting

Let

\[
\pi:S\to\mathbf P^1
\]

be a rational elliptic surface over \(\mathbf Q\) with section \(O\), twelve
irreducible \(I_1\) fibres, trivial torsion, and split Mordell--Weil lattice
\(E_8\). Let

\[
f:C\to\mathbf P^1
\]

be a cyclic cubic cover of genus one, totally ramified over three smooth fibres.
The base-changed surface \(S_C\to C\) has

\[
\chi(\mathcal O_{S_C})=3
\]

and no reducible fibres.

Let \(\sigma\) generate the deck group. Suppose a section \(P\in S_C(C)\)
has an orbit of size three and that its image on \(S\) is a genus-one
trisection \(D\). Put

\[
m=D\cdot O\ge0.
\]

## The trisection class

Using

\[
\operatorname{NS}(S)=\langle O,F\rangle\oplus E_8(-1),
\qquad O^2=-1,\quad O\cdot F=1,
\]

write

\[
D=3O+(m+3)F+v.
\]

Because \(K_S=-F\), adjunction for \(p_a(D)=1\) gives \(D^2=3\). Hence

\[
\boxed{\|v\|_{E_8}^2=6(m+1).}
\]

For \(m=0\), the classes are indexed by the norm-six shell of \(E_8\). Its
cardinality is

\[
240\sigma_3(3)=6720.
\]

More generally, the shell for intersection number \(m\) has

\[
\boxed{240\sigma_3(3(m+1))}
\]

vectors.

## Orbit heights are independent of the shell after projection

The three conjugate sections

\[
P_0=P,\qquad P_1=\sigma P,\qquad P_2=\sigma^2P
\]

meet pairwise at the three ramification points. Therefore

\[
P_i\cdot P_j=3\qquad(i\ne j).
\]

Since the pulled-back surface has \(\chi=3\) and no reducible fibres, Shioda's
height formula gives

\[
\langle P_i,P_i\rangle=6+2m,
\qquad
\langle P_i,P_j\rangle=2m\quad(i\ne j).
\]

Let

\[
T=P_0+P_1+P_2
\]

be the trace, and let

\[
q_i=P_i-\frac{T}{3}
\]

be the orthogonal projection to the nontrivial character space. Then

\[
\langle T,T\rangle=18(m+1)
\]

and, independently of \(m\),

\[
\boxed{
\langle q_i,q_i\rangle=4,
\qquad
\langle q_i,q_j\rangle=-2\quad(i\ne j).
}
\]

Thus every cyclic genus-one trisection orbit contributes a projected
\(A_2(2)\) plane. Equivalently, the difference lattice generated by
\(P_0-P_1\) and \(P_1-P_2\) is \(A_2(6)\):

\[
\operatorname{Gram}(A_2(2))=
\begin{pmatrix}4&-2\\-2&4\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{Gram}(A_2(6))=
\begin{pmatrix}12&-6\\-6&12\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This corrects the earlier unscaled root-lattice fingerprint: on a surface with
\(\chi=3\) and no reducible fibres, every nonzero section has height at least
six. A rank-30 Mordell--Weil lattice cannot contain roots of norm two or four.
Norm four occurs only after orthogonal projection away from the invariant
\(E_8(3)\) lattice.

## The mod-3 trace code

Let \(v,w\in E_8\) be the trace vectors of two trisection orbits. For projected
vectors \(q_P,q_Q\),

\[
\langle q_P,q_Q\rangle
=
\langle P,Q\rangle-\frac{\langle v,w\rangle_{E_8}}{3}.
\]

Consequently, a packet has an integral projected Gram matrix precisely when
its trace residues are mutually orthogonal in

\[
E_8/3E_8.
\]

Every trisection trace has norm divisible by three, so its nonzero residue is
isotropic. The reduction of the norm-six shell has the following exact finite
geometry:

* \(E_8/3E_8\) is an eight-dimensional split quadratic space over
  \(\mathbf F_3\), of Witt index four;
* it has exactly \(2240\) nonzero isotropic vectors;
* each nonzero isotropic residue has exactly three norm-six lifts in \(E_8\);
* it has exactly \(2240\) maximal totally isotropic four-spaces;
* each maximal four-space contains \(80\) nonzero vectors and therefore
  exactly \(240\) norm-six trisection classes;
* each nonzero isotropic vector lies in \(80\) maximal four-spaces.

The counts follow from the hyperbolic quadric \(Q^+(7,3)\):

\[
(3^3+1)(3^4-1)=2240
\]

nonzero isotropic vectors, and

\[
2\prod_{i=1}^{3}(3^i+1)=2240
\]

maximal totally isotropic four-spaces.

## Determinant identity

Let \(M\) be the full Mordell--Weil lattice of the cubic pullback, let

\[
L=M^{\langle\sigma\rangle}\cong E_8(3),
\]

and let \(K\) be the orthogonal projection of \(M\) to the trace-zero rational
subspace. If the projected pairing is integral, then the orthogonal-projection
index identity gives

\[
\boxed{\det M=\det L\,\det K=3^8\det K.}
\]

A natural low-regulator comparison target for \(K\) is the rank-22 laminated
lattice usually denoted \(\Lambda_{22}\), normalized to minimum four and
determinant twelve. Realizing that projected lattice would predict

\[
\det M=12\cdot3^8=78732.
\]

This is a target fingerprint, not a realization theorem.

## Search consequence

The minimal search no longer treats all \(6720\) norm-six trisection classes
as an undifferentiated set. For the integral projected-lattice track:

1. enumerate the \(2240\) maximal isotropic trace codes;
2. restrict each code to its \(240\) norm-six trisection classes;
3. compute the four-dimensional system \(|D_v|\) for each class;
4. retain members whose cubic discriminant is a square and whose branch points
   lie over smooth fibres;
5. canonicalize the resulting cyclic genus-one covers;
6. group trisections that define the same cover;
7. test Hermitian rank, ternary gluing, regulator, and saturation.

Eleven independent cyclic orbits over one cover would contribute twenty-two
trace-zero directions. Together with the invariant \(E_8(3)\), this is the
first degree-three mechanism capable of forcing generic rank thirty.
