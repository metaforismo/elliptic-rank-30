\section{Exact formulation and reproduced baseline}

\subsection{The theorem that would solve the challenge}

The required theorem is only a lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Target certificate]
There exists a nonsingular elliptic curve $E/\Q$ and rational points
$P_1,\ldots,P_{30}\in E(\Q)$ such that
\[
  \sum_{i=1}^{30}n_iP_i=O,\qquad n_i\in\Z,
\]
implies $n_1=\cdots=n_{30}=0$.
\end{theorem}

Exact rank $30$ is optional.  An upper bound, analytic or arithmetic, is not
needed once thirty listed points have been proved independent.

\subsection{Rank terminology}

The following quantities must not be interchanged.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.27\textwidth}p{0.67\textwidth}}
\toprule
Quantity & Meaning \\
\midrule
\endhead
Algebraic rank & $\rank_{\Z}E(\Q)$. \\
Analytic rank & $\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)$.  Equality with algebraic rank is the Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in general. \\
$\ell$-Selmer rank & The dimension of the Selmer group after subtracting rational $\ell$-torsion.  It equals the Mordell--Weil rank plus a possible $\Sha[\ell]$ contribution. \\
Generic rank & $\rank E(\Q(t))$ for an elliptic surface over $\mathbf P^1$. \\
Specialization rank & $\rank E_{t_0}(\Q)$ for a nonsingular rational fibre. \\
Rank jump & The rank gained beyond the specialized generic subgroup. \\
Visible rank & The rank of the subgroup generated by points actually found. \\
Saturated rank & The rank of a subgroup together with a proof that no hidden prime division enlarges it inside its rational span. \\
Exact rank & A matching rigorous lower and upper bound. \\
Conditional upper bound & An upper bound depending explicitly on GRH, BSD, or another stated conjecture. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

For every prime $\ell$, the exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow E(\Q)/\ell E(\Q)
\longrightarrow \operatorname{Sel}^{(\ell)}(E/\Q)
\longrightarrow \Sha(E/\Q)[\ell]
\longrightarrow0
\]
explains why a large Selmer rank is not a lower bound for the Mordell--Weil
rank.

\subsection{The current rank-$29$ record}

The reproduced baseline uses the integral generalized Weierstrass equation
\[
E_{29}:\quad y^2+xy=x^3-Ax+B,
\]
where the exact integers are displayed in line-broken decimal form:
\[
\begin{aligned}
A={}&2700618324163092221843465214529\cdot10^{31}\\
   &+7453784768054621836357954737385,\\[2pt]
B={}&552580585513423764757366995911\cdot10^{62}\\
   &+1819182152106703253507960837240\cdot10^{31}\\
   &+4779149413277716173425636721497.
\end{aligned}
\]
The repository stores the complete twenty-nine rational points in
\texttt{points.json}.

The dependency-free verifier performs exact substitution, checks
nonsingularity, and proves trivial rational torsion using good reductions
\[
\#E(\F_{19})=28,\qquad
\#E(\F_{23})=32,\qquad
\#E(\F_{67})=83.
\]
Their greatest common divisor is one.

For independence, the points are mapped to
\[
\prod_{p\in S}E(\F_p)/2E(\F_p)
\]
for twenty-two good primes.  The resulting binary matrix has rank twenty-nine.
If an integral relation existed, dividing its coefficients by their largest
common power of two would produce a nonzero binary relation, a contradiction.
Thus
\[
\boxed{\rank E_{29}(\Q)\ge29}
\]
unconditionally.

Independent SageMath and Magma computations reproduce the canonical-height
matrix and a nonzero regulator, and the listed subgroup is tested for
$p$-saturation for every prime $p<4096$.  These computations strengthen the
certificate but are not substitutes for the exact finite-reduction proof.
The published exact-rank upper statement remains clearly marked as
conditional.
